# Indie games playbook

Read when the product is a game. Apply each section in the matching phase of SKILL.md. For most PC games, the first win is **wishlists** (they drive visibility and launch sales), then sales and reviews. For mobile, it's installs and retention; for web games, plays and returning players.

## Phase 1: Discovery
- The **store page** is the front door (Steam, itch.io, App Store, Google Play, console stores): capsule art, trailer (the first seconds matter most), screenshots, short description, tags, release date or "coming soon", demo availability.
- Is there a **playable build** (demo, playtest, jam version)? Playability is the strongest proof for games.
- Genre, comparable titles ("X meets Y"), platform, price, release window, team size, and whether there's a publisher.
- Current traction: wishlists, followers, Discord members, mailing list, demo players, reviews.

## Phase 2: Extra interview questions
- Release date or window (firm or flexible)? Is a demo planned?
- Current wishlists, followers, Discord size, mailing list?
- Budget for a trailer, capsule art, events, ads?
- Comfortable streaming, posting devlogs, going to events?

## Phase 3: Extra research
- 5–10 **comparable games** (same genre, similar scope, released in the last 2–3 years): price, launch timing, review count (a rough proxy for sales; label any estimation method), what their store page and trailer do well.
- **Genre communities:** genre subreddits, Discords, forums, curators on the store, YouTubers and streamers who play this genre (smaller creators reply more often).
- **Events:** store festivals and themed sales (check eligibility and deadlines on the store's official pages), online showcases, local and regional game events, game jams.
- Wishlist and launch benchmarks: cite sources (developer post-mortems and industry write-ups exist; don't make numbers up).

## Phase 4: Extra readiness checks
- Capsule art readable at small size; trailer shows gameplay in the first seconds; screenshots show the core loop; tags match what players of comparable games search.
- A demo or playtest that ends wanting more, with a wishlist/follow call to action.
- A place to gather fans (Discord or mailing list) with a link from the store page and the game.
- Press kit page (description, facts, trailer, screenshots, logo, contact).

## Phase 6: Pricing
Price against comparable games of similar length and quality; plan the launch discount and the regional pricing defaults of the store; free demo vs. paid early access trade-offs.

## Phase 7: Game channels (score with the same matrix)
- **Store festivals and events** (big visibility spikes for games with a demo; plan around deadlines).
- **Streamers and YouTubers:** send keys to creators who play the genre, with a short pitch and a key-request link; track who played.
- **Short gameplay clips** on TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram, X, Reddit (GIF-worthy moments).
- **Devlogs** on one platform where genre fans gather.
- **Discord community** and playtests.
- **Press and curators:** small sites and store curators for the genre; a press kit and a short email.
- **Game jams and bundles** for visibility and feedback.
- **Paid ads** usually only once the store page converts well and the budget allows.

## Phase 8: Extra assets
Store page copy (short and long description, tags, feature bullets), trailer shot list (gameplay first, the hook in the first seconds), capsule art brief, streamer/key outreach message, press email, devlog post outlines, a festival/event calendar with deadlines, and a Discord welcome message.

## Metrics
Wishlists (and wishlist adds per day after each push), store page visits → wishlist conversion, demo players and median playtime, follower and Discord growth, creator coverage, then sales, refunds and review score after launch.
