# Validate mode: before (or while) building

Use when the founder only has an idea, a prototype, or is unsure people want it. Goal: in 2–3 weeks and very little money, a clear **go / change / stop** decision based on what real people did, not what they said.

## 1. Write the bet (5 minutes)
Ask, in one batch: who it's for, the painful problem, what they use today, and what they'd pay (or do) for a fix. Then write:
- **Problem hypothesis:** "[Who] struggles with [problem] at least [how often], and today they [workaround]."
- **Solution hypothesis:** "They'll [sign up / pay / pre-order / join the waitlist] for [solution] at [price]."
- **Riskiest assumption:** the one that kills the idea if wrong. Test that first.

## 2. Quick desk research (5–10 searches)
- Are people already complaining or asking about this? Collect 5–10 real quotes with links (use Targets mode's search recipes).
- Who already solves it, and at what price? Competition is a good sign (people pay); no one at all needs an explanation.
- Rough reachable market: how many of the ICP can the founder actually reach in named places?

## 3. Pick 1–2 tests that fit their hours
| Test | What it proves | Effort | Good-signal example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 problem interviews (script below) | The problem is real and frequent | 5–8h | 6/10 describe it unprompted and have tried to fix it |
| Smoke-test landing page + waitlist | People want it enough to leave an email | 3–5h | 10–20% of targeted visitors join |
| Fake door / pre-order | People will pay | 4–6h | a handful of pre-orders or deposits from strangers |
| Concierge / manual version | The outcome is valuable even done by hand | varies | users come back and ask for more |
| Post in a community asking about the problem (not pitching) | Pain and language | 1h | many replies sharing the same pain |

Set the **threshold before running the test** and write it down. Benchmarks vary a lot; if you cite one, source it.

## 4. Problem interview script (15–20 min, no pitching)
1. "Tell me about the last time you had to [do the job]." 2. "What was hardest about it?" 3. "Why was that hard?" 4. "What have you tried to fix it? What did it cost?" 5. "What don't you love about those solutions?" 6. "If you had a magic wand?" 7. "Who else should I talk to?"
Rules: ask about past behavior, not opinions about the future; don't describe your idea until the end, if at all; compliments ("cool idea!") are not data. Note quotes word for word.

## 5. Assets
Write only what the chosen tests need: interview outreach message (asking for advice, not selling), the smoke-test landing copy (headline, 3 benefits, waitlist form, honest "launching soon"), and the community post.

## 6. Decide
Fill a short scorecard in `marketing/validation.md`: hypothesis | test | threshold | result | verdict. Then:
- **Go:** switch to Plan mode (the waitlist becomes the first targets).
- **Change:** adjust the who, the problem or the offer, and test again (max 2 more rounds).
- **Stop:** say it kindly and plainly; that's a win, it saved months.

Also write a short `PLAN.md` (Phase 9 shape) for the validation weeks, so the founder has one page to follow, and offer exports.
